# NY micro-MSO MSA — Snowflake outline

**Status:** Outline + purpose notes + intended-meaning paragraphs + fill-in dictionary + worked example + role cards + conflict table + **first prose draft**. Not legal advice. Product home: [business-ideas-ny-mso-msa-synthesis.md](business-ideas-ny-mso-msa-synthesis.md). Draft: [business-ideas-ny-mso-msa-v1-draft.md](business-ideas-ny-mso-msa-v1-draft.md)

**Deal shape this template is for**

- Two entities: licensed **PLLC or PC** (the “Practice”) + non-professional MSO LLC (or GBC)
- Max ~3 people across both entities
- Flat monthly MSO fee (no % of revenue/collections)
- No investors, no practice purchase, no friendly-PC/PLLC stock or membership restriction in v1

**Packet to attach (not MSA sections)**

- Signed intake (owners, licenses, bank accounts, PHI Y/N)
- Filled Schedule A (services)
- Filled Schedule B (fee)

**Research notes used in this pass** (counsel still confirms; statutes change)

- NYSED: a GBC may provide non-professional services to licensees (e.g. scheduling, billing) if clinical vs admin stay distinct; a **PC may not** be a management company; a **PLLC may not** be a management company. [How to file a professional service entity](https://www.op.nysed.gov/corporate/disclaimer/how-to-file)
- Fee-splitting: 8 NYCRR **29.1(b)(4)** (unprofessional conduct for Title VIII professions, including social work and mental health counseling) bars sharing professional fees with outsiders and bars pay for space/facilities/equipment/personnel that is a **percentage of, or otherwise dependent on**, the licensee’s income or receipts. Education Law § 6509(9); additional health-profession overlay in § 6509-a for listed professions. [NYSED corporate practice](https://www.op.nysed.gov/corporate/corporate-practice-professions) · [Regents Part 29](https://www.op.nysed.gov/title8/rules-board-regents/part-29)
- NY courts have refused to enforce %‑of‑revenue practice deals as illegal fee-splitting (e.g. *Advanced Dental of Ardsley, PLLC v. Brown*).
- Commentators: MSO should not control Practice bank accounts, patient-care decisions, or clinical hiring. [Stevens & Lee](https://www.stevenslee.com/health-law-observer-blog/new-york-corporate-practice-of-medicine-and-its-impact-on-medical-spas/)
- HIPAA: if MSO creates/receives/maintains/transmits PHI for the Practice, it is a business associate and needs a BAA even if the same humans own both entities.

## 1. Parties and recitals

### 1.1 Parties

**Intended meaning:** The agreement is between two named legal entities, not two people. One is a NY PLLC or PC (the Practice); the other is an ordinary LLC or corporation (the MSO). After those names and types are filled, “Practice” and “MSO” mean those entities, and EINs exist so money and tax forms follow the entities.

- Legal name, entity type (**PLLC or PC**), formation state, and principal address of the professional entity  
  *Purpose: so the contract names the licensed firm that actually practices, and so later clauses can stay entity-type agnostic after this box is filled.*

- Legal name, entity type (ordinary LLC or corporation — not PLLC/PC), formation state, and principal address of the MSO  
  *Purpose: so the admin company is a non-professional entity. NYSED says a PC and a PLLC may not serve as a management services corporation.*

- Short defined terms: “Practice” (the named PLLC or PC) and “MSO”  
  *Purpose: so the rest of the form can say Practice/MSO instead of repeating PLLC vs PC.*

- Optional: EIN of each (or “to be inserted”)  
  *Purpose: so invoices, W-9s, and bank transfers match the legal entities, not a DBA or a person’s name.*

### 1.2 Recitals — what each entity is

**Intended meaning:** Professional services and licensed ownership sit only in the PLLC or PC. The MSO is a non-professional company that does not practice, is not a PC or PLLC, and is not a substitute professional firm.

- Practice is a NY professional entity (**PLLC or PC**) that provides licensed professional services  
  *Purpose: to put on the face of the agreement that professional services live only in the licensed entity.*

- Practice is owned only by licensed professionals authorized to own that PLLC or PC  
  *Purpose: NY professional-entity ownership is license-restricted; a recital that non-licensees own the Practice would contradict the whole structure.*

- MSO is a non-professional LLC or corporation that does not provide professional services  
  *Purpose: NYSED allows a GBC to provide non-professional services (scheduling, billing, etc.) only if that line is bright.*

- MSO is not a PC or PLLC and is not authorized to practice the profession  
  *Purpose: same NYSED rule — neither a PC nor a PLLC may act as the management company for other practices / as a general MSO.*

### 1.3 Recitals — why they are contracting

**Intended meaning:** Practice is buying listed admin help. MSO is not joining the professional firm, not delivering care, and not gaining clinical control. Recitals explain why the deal exists; they do not add powers beyond the later operative sections.

- Practice wants non-clinical administrative support  
  *Purpose: states the legitimate reason for paying an outsider without implying the outsider is joining the professional firm.*

- MSO will provide only the services listed later (not clinical care)  
  *Purpose: recitals should not expand scope beyond Schedule A.*

- Arrangement is intended to keep clinical control with the Practice  
  *Purpose: NY scrutiny is about who actually controls care, hiring of clinicians, and the Practice’s money — not just letterhead.*

- Recitals are background, not a hidden grant of extra powers  
  *Purpose: so a fluffy “MSO will support the practice” sentence cannot later be read as authority over clinical or financial control.*

### 1.4 Defined terms (short list only)

**Intended meaning:** A short glossary pins start date, the Schedule A services, the Schedule B dollar fee, clinical vs non-clinical staff, PLLC-or-PC as a fill-in, and PHI if needed — so later sections cannot redefine “services” or “fee” as whatever the parties happen to do.

- Effective Date  
  *Purpose: one start date for services, fee, and term so nothing is oral or retroactive by accident.*

- Management Services (points to Schedule A)  
  *Purpose: “services” in the body always means the checked list, not whatever the parties do in real life.*

- Management Fee (points to Schedule B)  
  *Purpose: one defined dollar obligation, not “whatever the MSO needs this month.”*

- Professionals / clinical staff vs non-clinical staff  
  *Purpose: hire/fire and supervision rules split on this line; blur it and the MSO starts looking like it runs the clinic.*

- Practice entity type: PLLC **or** PC (filled from 1.1; rest of form is entity-type agnostic)  
  *Purpose: one form for both; NYSED management-company ban and 29.1 fee-splitting apply to professional firms generally, not only medicine PCs.*

- PHI (if used later)  
  *Purpose: so Path A vs Path B in § 8.2 uses the HIPAA term, not vague “patient info.”*

## 2. Engagement and term

### 2.1 Engagement

**Intended meaning:** Practice hires MSO as a vendor to perform only Management Services. MSO accepts on those terms. Default for v1 is non-exclusive unless the parties choose exclusive for the checked admin lines.

- Practice engages MSO to perform Management Services  
  *Purpose: Practice is the client; MSO is the vendor. Not a partnership and not a sale of the practice.*

- MSO accepts, subject to this agreement  
  *Purpose: MSO’s duties are only those in this paper, not implied “run the business.”*

- Exclusive vs non-exclusive for the listed admin services (pick one default)  
  *Purpose: exclusive is simpler for a 2–3 person shop; non-exclusive reduces “MSO owns ops” optics. Counsel picks the default. v1 lean: **non-exclusive** unless they only want one bookkeeper.*

### 2.2 Independent contractor

**Intended meaning:** This is a vendor relationship, not a partnership, employment, or implied agency. MSO cannot bind Practice on clinical, payer, or professional matters except where this agreement later says so in a narrow, written way.

- MSO is not a partner, joint venturer, or employee of Practice  
  *Purpose: fee-splitting and corporate-practice analysis treat “we’re really one firm” as a problem; this sentence is the first paper wall.*

- MSO is not the Practice’s agent except where this agreement expressly says so  
  *Purpose: stops MSO from signing payer contracts, leases, or clinical policies in the Practice’s name by implied authority.*

- No authority to bind Practice on clinical, payer, or professional matters  
  *Purpose: those are the reserved powers in § 4; this is the engagement-level echo so they cannot be waived by a general “MSO may act for Practice” clause.*

### 2.3 Initial term

**Intended meaning:** Services and the flat fee start on a written Effective Date and run a stated period (default 12 months), not an open-ended “until we stop.” Nothing is oral or retroactive by accident.

- Start date = Effective Date  
  *Purpose: fee and services do not start before the writing is signed (NY: fee should be agreed in writing before services).*

- Length (default: 12 months)  
  *Purpose: a stated term shows a real vendor relationship. Twelve months is a common commercial default (also the usual Stark personal-services floor if Medicare/Medicaid later matters). Micro-deal can be shorter if counsel agrees.*

- End date or “through [date]”  
  *Purpose: no open-ended “until we feel like it,” which looks like ownership.*

### 2.4 Renewal

**Intended meaning:** The deal can auto-renew in 12-month steps if nobody timely opts out. Renewal does not change the fee or secretly convert it into a share of receipts; fee changes need a written Schedule B amendment.

- Auto-renew for successive 12-month periods unless a party gives notice  
  *Purpose: small shops will forget to re-sign; auto-renew keeps a written deal in force without implying perpetuity.*

- Notice period (e.g. 30 or 60 days before term end)  
  *Purpose: enough time to move bookkeeping/IT without a hostage situation.*

- Renewal does not change the fee unless Schedule B is amended in writing  
  *Purpose: blocks “we grew, so the fee is now 15% of collections” at renewal.*

### 2.5 Termination

**Intended meaning:** Either party can exit for convenience with notice, or for cause after a cure window. Hard events (loss of license, insolvency) end it immediately. On exit, MSO stops and loses admin access; Practice pays only earned unpaid flat fees — no trailing cut of future collections.

- Termination for cause (material breach + cure period)  
  *Purpose: if MSO starts controlling the bank account or clinical hiring, Practice can exit after notice/cure — or immediately if listed as a hard event.*

- Termination for convenience (either party, with notice)  
  *Purpose: micro-deals among 2–3 people need an off-ramp that is not a fight over “cause.”*

- Immediate termination for specified hard events (loss of license, insolvency — list, don’t invent extras)  
  *Purpose: if the Practice cannot legally practice, the MSO relationship should stop, not morph into the MSO “keeping the clinic open.”*

- Effect of termination: MSO stops services; Practice pays earned unpaid flat fees; wind-down of admin access  
  *Purpose: MSO cannot keep EHR/bank access as leverage; Practice cannot stiff earned flat fees. No trailing % of future receipts (that is the dental-APA fact pattern NY courts have refused to enforce).*

## 3. Services (Schedule A)

### 3.1 How Schedule A works

**Intended meaning:** Scope is the checked boxes plus any custom line actually filled in. Unchecked work is out even if someone mentioned it in conversation. There is no “and anything else reasonably required” catch-all.

- Only checked items are in scope  
  *Purpose: NYSED’s example is specific non-professional services (scheduling, billing). A vague mandate looks like the MSO is the practice.*

- Unchecked items are out of scope even if mentioned in marketing or conversation  
  *Purpose: stops “but you said you’d handle credentialing” from expanding control without a fee amendment.*

- Custom lines allowed only if filled in on the schedule  
  *Purpose: one escape hatch for a real extra task, still written and priced, not oral.*

- No catch-all (“and all other services reasonably required”)  
  *Purpose: that phrase is how templates give the MSO de facto control of everything.*

### 3.2 Candidate service lines (checkboxes)

**Intended meaning:** Each line is optional admin work a non-professional company may do (books, non-clinical payroll/HR, lease admin, IT/EHR admin, marketing, scheduling systems, credentialing support, compliance calendars). None of them include practicing, setting patient fees, signing payer contracts, or deciding clinical competency. Marketing must attribute clinical claims to the Practice.

- Bookkeeping / accounting (non-clinical)  
  *Purpose: classic GBC-to-licensee service. Books of the Practice stay the Practice’s books.*

- Payroll and benefits admin for **non-clinical** staff  
  *Purpose: payroll processing is admin; putting clinicians on the MSO payroll is how MSOs swallow the professional firm.*

- HR admin (PTO, onboarding paperwork) excluding clinical competency  
  *Purpose: paperwork vs “you’re not clinically competent / you’re fired” — the second is Practice-only.*

- Facilities / lease **administration** (not “MSO is landlord” unless a later module)  
  *Purpose: paying the landlord or tracking the lease is admin. MSO holding the lease is an asset deal we are not putting in v1. 29.1(b)(4) specifically flags % rent for space as fee-splitting.*

- IT / EHR **administration** (access, vendors, backups — not clinical documentation decisions)  
  *Purpose: hosting/admin is common; deciding what goes in the chart is clinical. If MSO touches PHI, Path B + BAA in § 8.*

- Marketing / website / social (clinical claims attributed to Practice)  
  *Purpose: ads that sound like the MSO “provides therapy” are unlicensed practice / holding out. Copy must name the Practice and licensees.*

- Scheduling systems / front desk ops (Practice sets clinical priorities)  
  *Purpose: NYSED explicitly lists scheduling as a GBC service. Practice still decides who is seen, waitlists, and clinical urgency.*

- Credentialing and payer-enrollment **support** (Practice signs; MSO assembles)  
  *Purpose: packet assembly is admin; the Practice is the enrolled provider. MSO signing payer contracts is reserved-power violation.*

- Compliance **support** (calendars, checklists — not legal advice)  
  *Purpose: reminders are admin. The template must not pose as a legal opinion or NYSED authorization.*

### 3.3 Per-checked-item detail

**Intended meaning:** Every checked box gets a concrete deliverable, a cadence, and an MSO contact so the fee is paying for identifiable work, not a vague right to “run the office.”

- One-line deliverable  
  *Purpose: FMV and 29.1 analysis ask “what did they actually pay for?” A checkbox with no deliverable is a fee in search of a service.*

- Cadence (weekly / monthly / as-needed)  
  *Purpose: same — a monthly books close is a real service; “as needed forever” is not.*

- Who at MSO is the contact (name or role)  
  *Purpose: in a 2–3 person shop this is usually one human; naming them makes the vendor relationship concrete.*

### 3.4 Changes to services

**Intended meaning:** Adding or dropping services requires a written Schedule A change. Extra work does not automatically change the fee unless Schedule B is also amended — and that amendment stays a flat dollar amount, not a percentage of collections.

- Add/remove services only by written amendment to Schedule A  
  *Purpose: scope creep is how ops drift into CPOM problems.*

- Fee does not auto-change unless Schedule B is also amended  
  *Purpose: more work can mean a new **flat** fee, never “take a bigger slice of collections.”*

## 4. Exclusions and clinical authority

### 4.1 Clinical services MSO must not perform

**Intended meaning:** MSO does not diagnose, treat, write clinical protocols, supervise licensees, set patient professional fees, or direct referrals/payer mix as a clinical or volume matter.

- Diagnosis, treatment, clinical protocols, supervision of licensees  
  *Purpose: that is the practice of the profession. NYSED: GBC may not exercise judgment over delivery of professional services or employ people who offer those services to the public.*

- Setting or changing fees charged to patients for professional services  
  *Purpose: professional-fee schedules are Practice decisions; MSO-set patient prices look like the MSO is selling the professional service.*

- Directing referrals or payer mix as a clinical matter  
  *Purpose: 29.1 also bars paying/receiving consideration for referrals. MSO “send all Medicaid to X” or volume quotas is a red flag even in a tiny shop.*

### 4.2 Authority reserved to Practice

**Intended meaning:** Practice alone hires/fires clinicians (and decides clinical competency), owns clinical records, signs payer and professional-service contracts, and holds professional judgment and standard of care.

- Hire, fire, and clinical-competency decisions for licensed professionals and clinical support  
  *Purpose: repeatedly listed by NY healthcare counsel as a CPOM tripwire if the MSO holds this power.*

- Ownership and control of medical / clinical records  
  *Purpose: records go with the professional relationship. MSO may host systems (with BAA) but must not own or ransom the chart.*

- Signing payer contracts and professional-service agreements  
  *Purpose: the enrolled provider and contracting professional entity is the PLLC or PC.*

- Professional judgment and standard of care  
  *Purpose: catch-all so any later “MSO best practices” clause cannot override clinical judgment.*

### 4.3 What MSO may do at the edge (narrow)

**Intended meaning:** MSO may source clinician candidates, recommend vendors/workflows, and prepare drafts. Practice decides whether to hire, whether to use a clinical workflow, and is the only signer.

- Recruit **candidates** for clinical roles; Practice decides whether to hire  
  *Purpose: NYSED allows a GBC to find professionals for employers. Sourcing ≠ selecting/firing.*

- Recommend vendors, software, or workflows; Practice decides clinical use  
  *Purpose: advice is admin; mandating a treatment protocol is not.*

- Prepare drafts for Practice signature; Practice signs  
  *Purpose: MSO can draft a payer form; binding the Practice requires Practice signature.*

### 4.4 NY-facing statements (outline, not legal conclusions)

**Intended meaning:** The parties intend to respect NY limits on unlicensed practice, professional-entity ownership, and fee-splitting for PLLCs and PCs alike. Nothing in the contract authorizes MSO to practice or to act as a PC or PLLC. If another clause fights reserved clinical authority, clinical authority wins. This is intent language, not nypllc’s legal opinion.

- Parties intend to comply with restrictions on corporate practice of the profession and fee-splitting (PLLC and PC alike)  
  *Purpose: 29.1(b)(4) is not medicine-only; it is unprofessional conduct for Title VIII professions including social work and MHC. PC and PLLC are both “professional firms.” This is intent language, not a legal opinion.*

- Contract language does not authorize MSO to practice the profession or to act as a PC or PLLC  
  *Purpose: restates the NYSED PC/PLLC-cannot-be-MSO rule from the other direction.*

- If a clause conflicts with reserved clinical authority, clinical authority wins  
  *Purpose: conflict rule so a broad indemnity or “MSO shall manage” leftover cannot eat § 4.*

## 5. Compensation (Schedule B)

### 5.1 Fee type (v1: one option)

**Intended meaning:** Practice pays one stated monthly dollar amount on a due date, by invoice or auto-pay from an account Practice controls. Late charges, if any, are ordinary commercial amounts, not a function of collections.

- Flat monthly Management Fee in a dollar amount  
  *Purpose: 29.1(b)(4) treats pay for space/facilities/equipment/personnel that depends on income or receipts as fee-splitting. A stated dollar amount is the v1-safe structure. Not a legal conclusion that any particular dollar is FMV.*

- Due date each month  
  *Purpose: vendor invoice rhythm, not “sweep whatever is left in the Practice account.”*

- Invoice vs auto-pay (pick a default)  
  *Purpose: invoice leaves a paper trail that Practice chose to pay; auto-pay is OK if Practice still controls the account and can stop it.*

- Late fee / interest: commercial, not punitive; not tied to collections  
  *Purpose: a late fee that is “10% of this month’s collections” reintroduces the forbidden formula.*

### 5.2 What the fee is not

**Intended meaning:** The fee is not a percentage of revenue, collections, or profits; not per-visit, per-patient, or per-referral; and not a residual claim on whatever cash is left in the Practice after expenses.

- Not a percentage of gross revenue, net revenue, collections, or profits  
  *Purpose: this is the core NY prohibition commentators and 29.1 text both hit. California-style %‑of‑gross MSO fees are the wrong template family.*

- Not per-visit, per-patient, or per-referral  
  *Purpose: those scale with professional volume; they look like sharing professional fees or paying for referrals (29.1(b)(3)–(4)).*

- Not a residual claim on Practice cash after expenses (except paying the stated flat fee)  
  *Purpose: “MSO gets everything left after clinician pay” is profit-sharing with an outsider, which NYSED describes as misconduct.*

### 5.3 What the fee covers

**Intended meaning:** The flat fee buys the checked Schedule A work. v1 has no pass-throughs unless Schedule B lists a receipted cost. A pass-through cannot be a hidden percentage of receipts.

- The checked Schedule A services only  
  *Purpose: fee must map to listed admin work, not to “the value of the practice.”*

- Pass-through costs (if any): listed, receipted, and not a hidden % of revenue  
  *Purpose: reimbursing a QuickBooks subscription at cost is not fee-splitting; “pass-through = 8% of receipts” is.*

- Default: no pass-throughs in v1 unless Schedule B lists them  
  *Purpose: keep first-review deals to one number.*

### 5.4 Fair market value posture

**Intended meaning:** The parties treat the fee as payment for the listed admin work. The template does not certify FMV. Independent valuation is optional, not something this form pretends already happened.

- Parties believe the fee is for admin services actually provided  
  *Purpose: federal AKS/Stark (if they ever bill federal programs) care that compensation not be a disguised referral payment. State fee-splitting cares that it not be a share of professional receipts.*

- Template does **not** opine that the number is FMV  
  *Purpose: nypllc is not a valuation firm; counsel/optional appraiser own that question.*

- Optional: parties may obtain independent FMV support; not required by this form  
  *Purpose: for a small flat fee, many attorneys will skip a formal study; the outline should not pretend a study happened.*

### 5.5 Changes to the fee

**Intended meaning:** The dollar fee changes only in a signed writing. Later versions may use a flat step-up table (e.g. $X then $Y). They may not auto-peg the fee to revenue, collections, or headcount-as-a-percentage.

- Written amendment only  
  *Purpose: same as services — no handshake “bump.”*

- No automatic bump tied to Practice revenue or headcount unless a later version adds a **flat** step-up table (still not %)  
  *Purpose: a scheduled $500 → $750 when they add a second clinician is still a flat fee. An auto “fee = 12% of collections when revenue exceeds X” is not.*

## 6. Financial separation

### 6.1 Practice accounts

**Intended meaning:** Professional receipts land in a bank account in the Practice’s name. Licensed owners keep visibility and ultimate control. Bookkeeping access is not the same as MSO signing authority on that account.

- Practice maintains at least one bank account in its own name  
  *Purpose: professional receipts belong to the professional firm. One shared “house account” in the MSO’s name is a classic control fact.*

- Patient payments, insurance, and professional fees deposit to Practice accounts  
  *Purpose: money from professional services must hit the PLLC/PC first, then the Practice pays the flat fee.*

- Practice (or its licensed owners) retains visibility and ultimate control  
  *Purpose: Stevens & Lee-type NY guidance: MSO should not control the professional entity’s bank accounts. View-only bookkeeping access ≠ signing authority on the Practice operating account.*

### 6.2 MSO accounts

**Intended meaning:** MSO has its own account. Practice pays the Management Fee across by invoice or transfer. Patient and insurance receipts are not MSO revenue.

- MSO maintains its own account  
  *Purpose: MSO is a separate taxpayer/vendor, not a DBA of the Practice.*

- Management Fee is paid **from** Practice **to** MSO by invoice/transfer  
  *Purpose: documents a purchase of admin services, not a split of the till.*

- MSO does not receive patient receipts as MSO revenue  
  *Purpose: if insurers pay the MSO, the MSO looks like the provider.*

### 6.3 What MSO must not do

**Intended meaning:** No daily sweep into an MSO-only account, no unilateral MSO control of the Practice operating account, and no mixing of patient receipts with MSO operating funds. v1 default: Practice owners are the bank signers.

- No daily sweep of Practice funds into an MSO-only account  
  *Purpose: PE MSAs often sweep to an MSO operating account; that is the opposite of this micro-template and a control fact NY commentary flags.*

- No unilateral control of Practice operating account  
  *Purpose: dual-admin on a bank login for bookkeeping is different from MSO-only control. v1 default: Practice owners are the signers.*

- No mixing of patient receipts with MSO operating funds  
  *Purpose: commingling destroys the entity wall and the fee-splitting story.*

### 6.4 Books and access

**Intended meaning:** Two entities, two ledgers. Practice may inspect enough records to confirm the flat fee (and any listed pass-throughs) was billed and paid as agreed. Tax treatment is for the parties’ accountants, not this MSA.

- Each party keeps its own books  
  *Purpose: two entities, two ledgers — even if one human does both jobs.*

- Practice may inspect records needed to confirm the flat fee was calculated/paid as agreed  
  *Purpose: for a flat fee this is mostly invoice history; still useful if pass-throughs exist.*

- Tax characterization left to the parties’ accountants (outline flag only)  
  *Purpose: MSA should not declare “this is guaranteed deductible” or S-corp treatment.*

## 7. Personnel and operations

### 7.1 Non-clinical personnel

**Intended meaning:** v1 default is Practice employs everyone, and MSO only provides services. If instead MSO employs a bookkeeper or receptionist, MSO may direct that person’s non-clinical work. Client relationships and clinical calendar priorities stay with Practice.

- Who employs front desk, bookkeeper, marketers (Practice vs MSO — pick a v1 default)  
  *Purpose: either can work if clinical staff stay on Practice. v1 lean for tiny shops: **Practice employs everyone**, MSO only provides services — fewest employment-law moving parts. Alternative: MSO employs the bookkeeper only.*

- MSO day-to-day direction of **non-clinical** work if MSO employs them  
  *Purpose: if MSO is the employer of the receptionist, MSO can manage that person’s admin tasks without managing therapists.*

- Practice still owns the client relationship and clinical calendar priorities  
  *Purpose: front desk scripts cannot turn the MSO into the treating practice.*

### 7.2 Clinical personnel

**Intended meaning:** Clinicians are employed or contracted by Practice, not MSO. MSO does not supervise professional services or set clinician compensation.

- Employed or contracted by Practice, not MSO  
  *Purpose: NYSED: GBC may not have employees who offer professional services to the public. Clinicians on MSO payroll is the wrong entity practicing.*

- MSO does not supervise professional services  
  *Purpose: supervision is a licensed-professional function.*

- Compensation of clinicians is a Practice decision  
  *Purpose: MSO-set clinician pay + volume targets is how “friendly PC” deals look like employment by the investor.*

### 7.3 Operations must match the paper

**Intended meaning:** Day-to-day operations must match this agreement. If MSO starts controlling clinical hiring or the Practice bank account, the parties amend or unwind — they do not “fix it in practice” while the paper stays pretty.

- Parties agree to run the business as this agreement describes  
  *Purpose: NY looks at substance. A perfect MSA with MSO-controlled accounts and MSO-fired therapists is still a bad structure.*

- If ops drift (MSO starts controlling clinical hiring or the bank account), they amend or unwind — not “fix it in practice silently”  
  *Purpose: gives the small group a rule for when the paper is no longer true.*

### 7.4 Policies and vendors

**Intended meaning:** MSO may propose non-clinical HR/IT/vendor policies. Clinical policies, informed consent, and standard of care stay with Practice.

- MSO may propose HR/IT/vendor policies for non-clinical functions  
  *Purpose: templates, password rules, vendor quotes — admin.*

- Clinical policies, informed consent, and standard of care stay with Practice  
  *Purpose: those documents are professional practice, not office admin.*

## 8. Records, PHI, and confidentiality

### 8.1 Clinical records

**Intended meaning:** Practice owns the charts. MSO gets access only as needed for checked admin work, if at all, and loses access at termination (no hostage records).

- Practice owns clinical / treatment records  
  *Purpose: the professional relationship and record custody sit with the PLLC or PC, not the MSO.*

- MSO access only as needed for listed admin services, if at all  
  *Purpose: HIPAA minimum-necessary; also avoids MSO “owning” the chart in practice.*

- Return or lock out MSO access on termination  
  *Purpose: no hostage records; MSO may retain copies only if the BAA (Path B) allows and law requires.*

### 8.2 PHI decision fork

**Intended meaning:** Path A (no PHI, no BAA) only if MSO truly never sees patient data. Path B (BAA exhibit required) if MSO handles PHI — including billing, EHR admin, or scheduling with names — even when the same people own both entities. Intake must match reality.

- Path A: MSO will **not** access PHI → say so; no BAA  
  *Purpose: possible if MSO only does lease admin / marketing without patient lists. Many “bookkeeping” MSOs actually see superbills — then Path A is a lie.*

- Path B: MSO **will** access PHI → BAA is a required exhibit; MSA points to it  
  *Purpose: HIPAA treats the MSO as a business associate when it handles PHI for the covered-entity Practice, including when the same people own both companies.*

- Intake questionnaire must match Path A or B  
  *Purpose: the fork is an ops fact, not a preference. Counsel should refuse Path A if Schedule A includes billing, EHR admin, or scheduling with patient names.*

### 8.3 Business confidentiality

**Intended meaning:** Ordinary vendor confidentiality for non-public business information, with exceptions for legal process and advisors. PHI duration lives in the BAA when Path B applies.

- Non-public business information of each party  
  *Purpose: ordinary vendor confidentiality, separate from PHI.*

- Reasonable exceptions (legal process, advisors, already public)  
  *Purpose: so confidentiality cannot block regulators, tax advisors, or the reviewing attorney.*

- Survival after termination (set a period)  
  *Purpose: PHI duties may last longer than a commercial confidentiality term; BAA handles PHI duration.*

### 8.4 Marketing and public statements

**Intended meaning:** The public is told the PLLC or PC (and named licensees) provide professional services. An MSO brand, if used, is admin-only and does not hold the MSO out as the treating practice.

- Public clinical claims attributed to Practice / named licensees  
  *Purpose: holding out: the public must not think the ordinary LLC is the therapist.*

- MSO brand vs Practice legal name: who may say what (short rule)  
  *Purpose: a consumer brand on the website with “services by [PLLC], members [names], licensed in NY” is the usual pattern; MSO name in the footer as “admin services” if used at all.*

## 9. Indemnity, insurance, and status

### 9.1 Indemnity

**Intended meaning:** MSO covers Practice for MSO’s admin mistakes and breaches. Practice covers MSO for professional-service / clinical claims. Neither indemnifies the other for willful misconduct or a knowing regulatory violation. Caps are a counsel call for v1.

- MSO indemnifies Practice for MSO’s admin negligence / breach  
  *Purpose: if MSO drops the payroll file or leaks a marketing list, MSO’s problem.*

- Practice indemnifies MSO for professional services / clinical claims  
  *Purpose: malpractice stays with the professional entity; MSO should not become the deep pocket for treatment claims just because it did bookkeeping.*

- Carve-out: no indemnity for a party’s willful misconduct or knowing regulatory violation  
  *Purpose: you cannot contract out of “we agreed to split fees on purpose.”*

- Caps/baskets: optional for v1 (flag for counsel; default may be uncapped or simple)  
  *Purpose: micro-deals often skip sophisticated cap tables; counsel decides if a cap would undermine the clinical/admin split.*

### 9.2 Insurance

**Intended meaning:** Practice carries professional liability. MSO carries CGL, plus cyber if Path B. Certificates on request; additional insured is optional for v1.

- Practice: professional liability / malpractice covering clinical services  
  *Purpose: the licensed firm carries the professional risk.*

- MSO: commercial general liability (and cyber if Path B PHI)  
  *Purpose: admin vendors who touch PHI are the cyber/BAA risk; CGL is ordinary vendor insurance.*

- Certificates on request; additional insured only if counsel wants it for v1  
  *Purpose: additional-insured requests can wait; not a CPOM issue.*

### 9.3 Limitation of liability (optional v1)

**Intended meaning:** A consequential-damages waiver is optional and commercial. It must not erase the clinical-vs-admin indemnity split (e.g. Practice eating MSO’s HIPAA breach, or MSO eating malpractice).

- Consequential-damages waiver or not (counsel call)  
  *Purpose: commercial, not regulatory. Flag only.*

- Must not swallow indemnity for clinical vs admin split  
  *Purpose: a waiver that makes Practice eat MSO’s HIPAA breach (or MSO eat a malpractice suit) defeats § 9.1.*

### 9.4 Status reminders

**Intended meaning:** Repeat that this is not a partnership and that employees belong to their employer of record — so ops cannot quietly put “everyone on the MSO.”

- Repeat: independent contractor, no partnership  
  *Purpose: belt-and-suspenders with § 2.2; partnership language is how fee-splitting arguments start.*

- Employees of one party are not employees of the other unless that party is the employer of record  
  *Purpose: stops “everyone works for the MSO” in the real world.*

- This MSA + schedules + BAA (if any) = entire agreement

**Intended meaning:** Ordinary severability and non-waiver. Severability is not a plan to salvage a percentage fee; v1 simply does not use one.

  *Purpose: ordinary.*

**Intended meaning:** Clinical exclusions, the “not a % of revenue” rule, confidentiality, indemnity, record ownership, and earned unpaid flat fees survive termination. No post-term earnout or trailing collections split.

### 10.7 Counterparts and authority

**Intended meaning:** E-sign is fine. The person who signs warrants they can bind that PLLC, PC, or ordinary LLC.

  *Purpose: PLLC member vs PC officer — whoever 1.1 says can sign that entity.*

## Fill-in dictionary (this snowflake pass)

Every future form blank. Allowed values are constrained by the research below. **Do not treat example dollars as FMV.**

### Research that constrains the blanks

- **HIPAA Path A vs B:** HHS lists accounting, billing, practice management, and EHR/IT support as business-associate functions when they involve PHI. A CPA/bookkeeper whose work includes patient-level AR, EOBs, or deposits that name patients is a business associate. [HHS business associates](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html). A **workforce member of the Practice** (W-2 of the PLLC/PC) is **not** a BA. An **MSO entity** hired to do that work **is** a BA even if the bookkeeper is the owner’s spouse. Appointment name + phone is already PHI. Path A is honest only if Schedule A has no patient-identifying work (e.g. lease admin + brand marketing with no lists). Janitorial-style *incidental* access is not a BA; routine books/scheduling is.
- **Related-party / same human both sides:** NY LLCL **§ 411** (and BCL analog for PCs) — a contract with an entity in which a manager has an interest is not automatically void if material facts are disclosed and the members/managers approve, or if the deal is shown fair. Micro-MSO deals are almost always related-party. Blanks must include a **disclosure + capacity** (two signature blocks: “as member of Practice” vs “as member of MSO”), not one scribble.
- **Practice bank signers:** PLLC members/managers and PC shareholders/officers/directors must be licensed in the profession ([NYSED consent process](https://www.cogencyglobal.com/blog/new-york-state-consent-process-for-professional-corporations-and-llcs/)). The MSA must **not** make the unlicensed MSO owner a signer on the Practice operating account. Banks use the entity’s own banking resolution; this form only *forbids* MSO unilateral control.
- **Venue:** “Courts of the State of New York” is jurisdiction, not venue. CPLR **501** needs a **named county** or CPLR **503** defaults to a party’s residence / principal office county. Blank: **County of Practice principal office.**
- **E-sign:** NY **ESRA** — electronic signatures have the same effect as wet ink for this kind of commercial contract ([NYS ESRA](https://nyc-business.nyc.gov/nycbusiness/description/accepting-electronic-signatures)).
- **Fee formula:** 8 NYCRR **29.1(b)(4)** still bans any blank that is “% of receipts” or otherwise dependent on Practice income — including late fees, pass-throughs, and renewal bumps.

## Role cards

Four actors. **Entities** are not humans; **humans** wear one hat per signature block. Research: LLCL **§ 1204 / § 1207** (PLLC practices only through licensees; members/managers must be licensed); BCL **§ 1507 / § 1508** (PC shares and officers/directors — licensees only, except DPC/CPA special statutes we are not using); Educ. Law **§ 6512** (unlicensed practice / holding out / aiding unlicensed practice = class E felony); **§ 6513** (unauthorized professional title); **§ 6509(7)** and 8 NYCRR **29.1(b)(10)** (improper delegation); NYSED MHC [Guideline 6](https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/mental-health-counselors/professional-practice/delegation-professional-practice-authorized-persons); 45 CFR **§ 160.103** (workforce vs business associate).

### Card A — Practice (the PLLC or PC)

| May | Must not |
|-----|----------|
| Provide professional services to the public through licensed individuals (LLCL § 1204 / PC analog) | Be owned, member-managed, or officer-directed by an unlicensed person (LLCL § 1207; BCL § 1508 — spouse/investor cannot be a member, manager, shareholder, director, or officer) |
| Employ or contract **clinicians** | Employ clinicians **through** the MSO or “lease” them from the MSO to serve the public |
| Own clinical records; enroll with payers; set professional fees charged to patients | Let the MSO set patient fees, sign payer contracts, or own/ransom the chart |
| Receive patient/insurance receipts in a Practice-named account; pay a **flat** Management Fee | Pay the MSO a % of receipts, per-visit, or “whatever is left” (8 NYCRR 29.1(b)(4)) |
| Hire/fire clinicians and decide clinical competency | Delegate diagnosis, psychotherapy, assessment-based treatment planning, or other restricted Title VIII acts to an unlicensed person — even “under supervision” (NYSED Guideline 6; Educ. Law § 6509(7)) |
| Be the HIPAA **covered entity** (typical small provider who bills electronically) | Treat the MSO as workforce if the MSO is a separate contracted entity doing BA work — that needs a BAA (45 CFR § 160.103: BA is *other than* a workforce member) |
| Hold professional liability insurance | Hold itself out as “managed by” an ordinary LLC in a way that the LLC appears to be the treating practice (§ 6512 holding out) |

### Card B — MSO (ordinary LLC or GBC)

| May | Must not |
|-----|----------|
| Provide **non-professional** services NYSED lists as OK for a GBC (scheduling, billing, finding candidates, office admin) if the clinical line stays bright | Be a PC or PLLC, or “serve as a management services corporation” in NYSED’s words — a professional entity may only manage **its own** professional services |
| Own ordinary-LLC membership (anyone, including unlicensed spouses) | Offer professional services to the public or hire licensees to do so as MSO employees (NYSED: GBC cannot hire a licensee to provide professional services to the public; § 6512) |
| Invoice a flat Management Fee; keep its own bank account | Receive patient receipts as MSO revenue; daily-sweep the Practice operating account; have unilateral signing authority on the Practice account |
| Touch PHI **as a business associate** if Path B + BAA (accounting, billing, calendar names, EHR admin) | Touch PHI with no BAA; use PHI for MSO marketing beyond what the BAA/Privacy Rule allow |
| Employ **non-clinical** staff (if that is the chosen H2) and direct their admin work | Employ therapists/social workers/MHCs to treat the Practice’s clients; supervise professional services; set clinician pay or volume quotas |
| Propose vendors, draft forms, recruit **candidates** | Hire/fire clinicians, decide competency, write clinical protocols, or bind Practice on payer/professional contracts |
| Use an MSO brand for *admin* in the footer | Use a professional title or hold the MSO out as able to practice (§ 6513 / § 6512); put “Jordan Admin LLC, LCSW” on the door |

### Card C — Licensed owner (human; e.g. Alex)

| May | Must not |
|-----|----------|
| Own 100% (or share only with other licensees in the **same** profession rules for that PLLC/PC) | Put a spouse, investor, or MSO on the Practice cap table or as manager/officer |
| Practice, use the professional title, be the public face of care | Aid or abet unlicensed practice, including letting the MSO owner “do a bit of counseling” or triage that is restricted (§ 6512; § 6509(7); 29.1 improper delegation) |
| Sign the MSA **as member/officer of Practice only**; be the Practice bank signer | Sign once for both entities without two capacities; give the unlicensed person sole Practice-account control |
| Also own the MSO (dual-hat variant) — still two signatures, related-party disclosure (LLCL § 411) | Assume dual-hat erases fee-splitting or CPOM; counsel should ask why two entities exist |
| Decide clinical hire/fire, records, payer contracts, patient fees | Rubber-stamp MSO decisions on those topics so the paper and the ops diverge |
| Employ the spouse as Practice **workforce** (W-2 receptionist) *instead of* MSO-provided books — then that human is not a BA **in that capacity** | Pretend Path A while the **MSO entity** still does patient-level books (the entity is still a BA) |

### Card D — Non-licensed owner (human; e.g. Jordan)

| May | Must not |
|-----|----------|
| Own and manage the **MSO** LLC 100%; be MSO bank signer | Own, manage, or be an officer/director/member of the Practice (LLCL § 1207; BCL § 1508). “Silent spouse on the PLLC” is not a v1 option |
| Do unlicensed-person work: scheduling, insurance forms, bookkeeping, marketing ops, assisting a client with a **form** (NYSED: functions any unlicensed person could perform) | Perform restricted professional acts (for MHC/LCSW lane: assessment/evaluation, diagnosis, assessment-based treatment planning, psychotherapy, treatment other than psychotherapy — Educ. Law Part Y / § 7701 / Art. 163). Cannot be saved by “Alex supervises me” |
| Appear in ads as office/admin contact for the MSO | Use “LCSW,” “therapist,” “counselor” (restricted titles) or hold out as able to practice (§ 6513 / § 6512) |
| Recruit clinician **candidates** for Practice to accept or reject | Decide who is hired/fired as a clinician or evaluate clinical competency |
| See PHI only through Path B + BAA **when acting for the MSO**, or as Practice workforce if actually employed by Practice | Access charts because “we’re married” with no workforce or BA status |
| Sign the MSA **as member of MSO only** | Be listed as Practice’s “managing partner,” co-owner of the practice, or signer who can empty the Practice operating account alone |

### Dual-hat and two-hat-same-house (ops)

| Situation | HIPAA | NY entity law | Template rule |
|-----------|--------|---------------|---------------|
| Spouse is W-2 of Practice, no MSO books | Workforce of CE; no BAA with the spouse | Fine; spouse still cannot own Practice | Do not check Schedule A bookkeeping **for the MSO** |
| Spouse’s MSO LLC is paid to do books | MSO entity is a **BA**; BAA required even though same household | Fine if MSO is ordinary LLC | Path B; Jordan signs as MSO |
| Same licensee owns Practice and MSO | CE vs BA still two entities if MSO handles PHI | Related-party (LLCL § 411) | Two signature blocks; counsel asks “why MSO?” |
| Unlicensed person on Practice bank as “convenience signer” | N/A | Not a member — but **control of receipts** is a CPOM fact | v1: **no**; licensed owner is the Practice signer |

## Conflict table

What to do when paper and life disagree. **NY courts will not rescue an illegal deal** — they dismiss contract *and* unjust enrichment and “leave the parties where their own acts have placed them” (*Advanced Dental of Ardsley, PLLC v. Brown*, 2d Dept 2024; *Linchitz Practice Mgt., Inc. v Daat Med. Mgt., LLC*, 2d Dept 2018 — practice-management note treated as pretext for fee-splitting). So the template’s job is to **refuse bad blanks**, not to write a clause that “fixes” a % fee later.

| # | Conflict | Why it hurts | Template / intake | If ops already drifted |
|---|----------|--------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| 1 | **Fee is % of collections / “what’s left after expenses”** (in MSA, side letter, or handshake) | Educ. Law fee-splitting; 29.1(b)(4). Courts refuse to enforce the paper *or* quantum meruit that depends on the illegal purpose (*Advanced Dental*; *Linchitz*). | **Hard stop.** Do not generate the form. | Unwind to a **flat** dollar going forward. Do not sue on the old %. Counsel — not this template — owns leftover money. |
| 2 | **Oral “you get 20%”** while MSA says flat | Entire-agreement is useless if they perform the oral deal; substance controls. Same unenforceability if the real deal is %. | Intake asks: any other compensation? **Must be none.** | Stop the % immediately; paper the true flat fee or stop MSO work. |
| 3 | **Mid-month termination** (convenience or cause) | Commercial, not regulatory — unless someone invents a trailing % of post-term receipts (*Advanced Dental* was a % of future revenue). | Pay **pro-rata earned flat fee** only (days served / days in month). Wind-down access. **No** earnout. | Invoice the stub month as F1 × days/30. Cut EHR/bank access on the end date. |
| 4 | **Practice cannot pay the flat fee** (slow month, not a % clause) | Unpaid vendor invoice is a contract debt if the fee itself is legal. Do **not** convert it to “pay us when collections come in” as a % — that mutates into 29.1(b)(4). | Invoice + optional late charge **only on unpaid Management Fee**. No collections-based default interest. | Accrue the **stated dollars**. Do not take a lien on receivables or a sweep. |
| 5 | **License suspended/revoked / sole licensee gone** | Practice cannot lawfully offer the profession. If MSO “keeps seeing clients,” that is § 6512 (unlicensed practice / aiding). Hard-event termination exists so the MSO does not become the clinic. | **Immediate** termination (X1). MSO stops. Records stay with Practice / successor licensee. | Stop professional services the same day. Admin wind-down only. |
| 6 | **Path A checked but Schedule A has books, billing, EHR, or scheduling with names** | HHS: accounting/billing/practice management + PHI = BA. OCR has fined **small** providers for disclosing PHI **without a BAA** even with no separate “hack” story (e.g. Center for Children’s Digestive Health ~$31k). Missing BAA is a violation whether or not a breach occurs. | **Hard stop.** Flip to Path B + attach BAA, or uncheck the PHI-touching boxes. | Execute BAA **before** more PHI flows. Do not keep Path A as a fiction. |
| 7 | **Spouse does books as W-2 of Practice *and* MSO is paid for the same books** | Double-pay plus confused HIPAA (workforce vs BA). | Pick one: H2 Practice workforce **or** S1 MSO books, not both for the same tasks. | Stop one channel; align PHI1. |
| 8 | **One signature for both entities** / “Alex signed for everybody” | NY wants capacity on the signature line; related-party deals need disclosure (LLCL § 411). One scribble looks like one firm. | **Two blocks**, titled. R1 checklist. | Re-execute with two capacities. Do not backdate a % into it. |
| 9 | **Unlicensed person listed as Google Business / Psychology Today / website “owner” or “therapist”** | § 6512 **holding out**; § 6513 **title**. Ads, profiles, and invoices are typical proof. NYSED OPD takes written complaints of unlicensed practice; AG can prosecute. | GBP and directory **Practice legal name + licensed owner**. MSO name only as admin vendor if at all. | Fix listings **now**. Jordan is office/admin, never “co-owner of the practice.” |
| 10 | **Catch-all Schedule A / MSO “runs the practice”** | NYSED: GBC may do listed non-professional services, not exercise professional judgment. Ops that match “MSO runs it” are CPOM facts even if recitals are pretty. | No catch-all. Exclusive only for **checked** lines. | Amend A to real tasks or unwind. |
| 11 | **MSO hires/fires clinicians or sets patient fees** | Reserved Practice powers; improper delegation (29.1(b)(10); Guideline 6). | Role cards C/D. Immediate cause if it happens. | Move decisions back to licensed owner the same week. |
| 12 | **Clinicians on MSO payroll treating the public** | GBC cannot offer professional services through employees (NYSED corporate practice). | H1 = Practice only. Hard stop. | Move employment to Practice; do not “second” them from MSO. |
| 13 | **Daily sweep / MSO-only control of Practice operating account** | Control of professional receipts is a CPOM/fee-split fact (Stevens & Lee-type NY guidance). | B2 = licensed Practice signers only. 6.3 bans sweep. | Restore Practice-named account + licensee signers. Reverse sweep if possible. |
| 14 | **MSO holds the lease and rent is % of receipts** | 29.1(b)(4) expressly flags % pay for **space**. v1 has no landlord module. | S4 = lease *admin* only, or N. | Convert rent to a **fixed** dollar or put lease in Practice name. |
| 15 | **MSO keeps EHR/charts after exit (“pay us or no records”)** | Records belong to Practice; hostage access is control + possible abandonment of care. HIPAA: BA returns/destroys per BAA. | 8.1 lockout + return. Survival of record-ownership. | Give Practice export **immediately**; fight the invoice separately as a **flat** stub, never as a condition of the chart. |
| 16 | **Same licensee owns both entities, fee looks like taking home “the profit”** | Two hats do not legalize a % or a drained PLLC. Related-party fairness (LLCL § 411). | Allowed as variant; counsel asks why MSO exists; fee still flat and mapped to S1–S9. | Document deliverables; do not treat MSO as a pocket for professional receipts. |
| 17 | **Unlicensed “convenience signer” on Practice bank** | Not a PLLC member (lawful) but **unilateral control of receipts** is the problem. | v1 **no**. | Remove signer; licensed owner only. |
| 18 | **MSO “triage” / “just talk to the new client until Alex is free”** | Restricted acts (Part Y / MHC-LCSW practice definitions). Aiding unlicensed practice (§ 6512, § 6509(7)). | Card D cannot. Cause + stop. | Stop. Only licensed/exempt persons do restricted acts. |
| 19 | **Venue blank = “New York courts”** | CPLR 501 needs a **county**; otherwise 503 fight. | V1 = county of Practice office. | Fill county in an amendment; low legal risk vs #1–#6. |
| 20 | **They want this form plus a practice-sale earnout** | That is *Advanced Dental*, not an MSA. | **Out of scope.** Different engagement; healthcare counsel. | Do not staple earnout language onto this template. |

**Severity key**

- **Hard stop** — do not emit a “compliant-looking” MSA; it will not save them in court or at NYSED/OCR.
- **Flip / amend** — same deal, honest blanks (Path B, two signatures, listings).
- **Commercial only** — stub-month math, unpaid flat invoice, venue county.
