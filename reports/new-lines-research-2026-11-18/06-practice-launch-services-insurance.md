### 3. New York Insurance Law: who may sell, solicit, receive compensation
Primary texts opened: nysenate.gov §2102 `[M]`; DFS OGC Opinions 06-11-23 and 05-04-16 on dfs.ny.gov `[M]`. §2114 and §2115 text from newyork.public.law (statute mirror) `[S]`.

- **Licensing requirement, §2102(a)(1)(A):** "No person, firm, association or corporation shall act as an insurance producer, insurance adjuster or life settlement broker in this state without having authority to do so by virtue of a license issued and in force pursuant to the provisions of this chapter." `[M]`
- **Receiving compensation, §2102(e)(1):** "No person shall accept any commission, service fee, brokerage or other valuable consideration for selling, soliciting or negotiating insurance in this state if that person is required to be licensed under this article and is not so licensed." (§2102(e)(2) permits renewal/deferred commissions only if licensed at time of sale.) `[M]`
- **Insurer payments, §2102 (e)(3) per nysenate text; a search summary labelled it (a)(3)):** insurer or licensed producer "may pay or assign commissions, service fees, brokerages or other valuable consideration to an insurance producer or to persons who do not sell, solicit or negotiate a contract of insurance in this state ... unless the payment would violate any provision of this chapter." `[M]` (subsection lettering to be confirmed)
- **Fees for evaluating policies, §2102(b)(3):** unlicensed persons may not "receive any money, fee, commission or thing of value for examining, appraising, reviewing or evaluating any insurance policy" (exceptions in (b)(4): NY attorneys in professional capacity; actuaries/CPAs without compensation; salaried insurer employees). `[M]`
- **Property/casualty (the malpractice, GL, cyber lines), §2115(a)(1):** "No insurer doing business in this state, and no agent or other representative thereof ... shall pay any commission or other compensation to any person, firm, association or corporation for acting as insurance agent in this state, except to a licensed insurance agent ..." Referral exemption: "'acting as insurance agent' shall not include the referral of a person to a licensed insurance agent or broker that does not include a discussion of specific insurance policy terms and conditions and where the compensation for referral is not based upon the purchase of insurance by such person." Page text shows this version in effect "until September 10, 2029", with a later version omitting the referral language. `[S]` (newyork.public.law; DFS opinion 05-04-16 quotes the same wording `[M]`)
- **Life / accident & health analog, §2114(a)(4):** referral to a licensed agent or broker excluded from restricted services, same two conditions (no policy-terms discussion; pay "not based upon the purchase of insurance by such person"). Page notes "Repealed September 10, 2029". `[S]`
- **DFS OGC Op. 06-11-23 (2006):** "A licensed insurance agent may not compensate a non-licensee for referrals contingent upon the placement of insurance." Paying a referral fee "based on a per-policy sold basis would violate § 2114(a)(4)" and the referrer would violate §2102. Non-licensee may be paid if pay is not tied to placement and no specific policy terms are discussed. `[M]`
- **DFS OGC Op. 05-04-16 (2005):** referral allowed if no policy-terms discussion and "the compensation for referrals is not based on whether a sale is made"; "The Insurance Law does not limit the amount of such compensation." Response #2 (gift card) withdrawn per Ch. 291 L.2012; response on referral fees not stated as withdrawn. §2324(a) bars "any valuable consideration or inducement of any kind" not in the policy (exception: merchandise not over $15). `[M]`
- **DFS OGC Op. 04-07-23 (2004):** per a search summary, rules apply both to referrals by unlicensed individuals and by prospective purchasers. Not opened. `[S]`
- Penalty section §2127 referenced in §2115 table of contents; text not retrieved. `[U]`
- Thimble's own affiliate page: payment "$30 per qualified lead", no agent license required, US-based affiliates only; a Thimble broker page (per search summary) says a P&C license is required for referral payments. `[M]` (affiliate page) / `[S]`

### 3b. Steps, exams, fees: NY property/casualty agent or broker (individual)
Sources: DFS page https://www.dfs.ny.gov/apps_and_licensing/agents_and_brokers/lic_app_pc_br (read Nov 18 2026) `[M]`; PSI Candidate Information Bulletin (NY DFS), "Updated 4/28/2023" (older, read from PDF) `[M]`.
- Prelicensing: Property and Casualty Agent/Broker 90 hours; Personal Lines 40 hours. Broker applicants with qualifying insurance employment may submit a Statement of Employer instead of the course; CPCU waives education and takes the 17-74 Property Casualty Laws and Regulations exam.
- Exam (PSI Services): Series 17-56 for P&C; 150 items, 2.5-hour limit; 70% to pass; exam fee $40 (non-refundable; forfeited if not taken within 1 year) (PSI bulletin 2023).
- Apply through NY LINX within 2 years of passing; license fee: DFS page says $80 for a licensing period over one year (half fee $40 for one year or less); PSI bulletin (2023) lists Property Casualty Agent/Broker $40 "for each year or fraction thereof". Licenses run up to 2 years, expiring on birthday by birth-year parity.
- Appointment: not required to obtain the agent license; notice of appointment must be filed within 15 days of agency-contract execution or first application, whichever is first; an agent with no appointment is inactive. Brokers represent the insured and need no appointment, though some carriers accept business only from appointed agents.
- Continuing education: 15 credits each biennial period after the first two years (PSI bulletin 2023).
- Entity (agency) license fees and requirements: PSI bulletin says call DFS at 800.342.3736; not retrieved. `[U]` Appointment fees charged by carriers: not found. `[U]` Fingerprinting: not mentioned on the DFS page I read. `[U]`
- Duplicate license print fee $15 (DFS licensing FAQ) `[M]`.
