## 3. Signing and liability

### Who signs (federal)
- "A tax return preparer is any person who prepares for compensation … all or a substantial portion of any return of tax or any claim for refund." §301.7701-15(a). [M] law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/301.7701-15
- "A signing tax return preparer is the individual tax return preparer who has the primary responsibility for the overall accuracy" of the return. §301.7701-15(b)(1). A nonsigning preparer prepares a substantial portion but is not the signer, §301.7701-15(b)(2). [M]
- Not a preparer: an individual providing "only typing, reproduction, or other mechanical assistance"; an employee preparing the return of their own employer. §301.7701-15(f)(1). [M]
- The individual signing preparer "shall sign the return … after it is completed and before it is presented to the taxpayer"; if unavailable, another preparer must review the entire preparation and then sign. Examples show the individual (not the firm) signs. §1.6695-1(b)(1), (b)(4) Ex. 1–4. [M] law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6695-1
- The page opened does not say a firm may sign in its own name. [M]

### Preparer penalties
- **§6694(a)** unreasonable position: greater of **$1,000** or 50% of income derived per return. **§6694(b)** willful/reckless: greater of **$5,000** or 75%. (Fixed by statute, not inflation-indexed in Rev. Proc. tables.) [M] law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6694
- **§6695** amounts, Rev. Proc. 2024-40 §.55 (returns filed in **2026**) and Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.54 (returns filed in **2027**) [M] irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-24-40.pdf, rp-25-32.pdf:

| (a) furnish copy to taxpayer | $65 / $32,500 | $65 / $33,000 |
| (b) sign return | $65 / $32,500 | $65 / $33,000 |
| (c) furnish identifying number (PTIN) | $65 / $32,500 | $65 / $33,000 |

- Taxpayer-side late-filing penalty for 1120-S (§6699(b)(1), per shareholder per month): $255 for returns due in 2026; $260 for 2027. [M] same Rev. Procs.

### EFIN (IRS e-file) for a firm whose owner is not credentialed — IRS Pub 3112 (Rev. 11-2026) [M] irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p3112.pdf, pp. 5–6
- Every Principal and Responsible Official must be a US citizen or lawful permanent resident, at least 18, and "Meet applicable state and local licensing and/or bonding requirements for the preparation and collection of tax returns."
- "Principals and Responsible Officials must be fingerprinted unless they provide professional status information" (attorney, CPA, enrolled agent, officer of a publicly held corporation, or banking official bonded and fingerprinted within two years). LPAs must be fingerprinted.
- Who is a Principal: sole proprietorship — "The individual owner"; partnership — each partner with 5% or more; corporation — "The President, Vice-President, Secretary, and Treasurer are each a Principal"; LLC/other — "an individual authorized to act for the entity in legal and/or tax matters. At least one such individual must be on the application."
- "A Responsible Official is an individual with authority over the Provider's IRS e-file operation at a location, is the first point of contact with the IRS…"
- Pub 3112 does **not** require a Principal or Responsible Official to be a CPA, EA or attorney; non-credentialed individuals are fingerprinted and pass a suitability check (credit, tax compliance, criminal background, prior e-file compliance). Approval "may take up to 45 days." No application fee is stated. [M]
- PTIN: required for anyone who prepares or assists for compensation (see round 1); nonsigning preparers included. [M round 1]

### New York (Tax Law §32) [M] nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/TAX/32
- "Tax return preparer" = "an individual who prepares a substantial portion of any return for compensation" (§32(a)(14)); includes "Employees of a tax return preparer or a commercial tax return preparation business" and "partners who prepare returns for clients of a partnership." Excludes attorneys, public accountants, EAs, CPAs and their supervised employees.
- "Commercial tax return preparation business" = "an entity that employs individuals who prepare tax returns" (§32(a)(4)).
- Each preparer and each facilitator "must register electronically with the department for that calendar year" (§32(b)(1)); the preparer or business "must ensure that the employee or prospective employee is properly registered" (§32(b)(2)(C)); commercial preparers pay $100/yr (§32(c)(1)).
- The statute text does not use the word "owner"; a non-preparing owner has no stated registration duty. [M]

### Data security
- FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR 314): "tax preparation firms" listed as financial institutions. Required: written information security program; a "Qualified Individual"; written risk assessment; encryption in transit and at rest; MFA "for anyone accessing customer information"; written report to board at least annually; notify FTC "no later than 30 days after discovery" of an event affecting 500+ consumers. Firms with fewer than 5,000 consumers are exempt from certain provisions only. [M] ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know
- IRS Pub 5708 (Rev. 8-2024), "Creating a Written Information Security Plan for your Tax & Accounting Practice": "Under the GLBA and Safeguards Rule… Your WISP must be written and accessible"; includes a sample template. [M] irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5708.pdf
- IRS Pub 5709: "Federal law, enforced by the Federal Trade Commission, requires professional tax preparers to create and maintain a written data security plan." [M] irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5709.pdf
- IRS Pub 4557 "Safeguarding Taxpayer Data" covers FTC Safeguards compliance and EFIN/PTIN monitoring. [M] irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4557.pdf
- IRS page: "Federal Trade Commission regulations require professional tax preparers to create and enact security plans." [M] irs.gov/tax-professionals/protect-your-clients-protect-yourself
- PTIN-renewal attestation about a security plan: not found on pages opened. [U]
